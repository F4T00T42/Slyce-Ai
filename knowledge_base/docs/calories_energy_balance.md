---
title: Calories and Energy Balance
source: https://www.cdc.gov/healthyweight
---
A calorie is a unit of energy. Body weight change is driven largely by energy
balance: the difference between calories consumed and calories expended. A
sustained calorie deficit (eating fewer calories than you burn) leads to weight
loss, while a surplus leads to weight gain.

Total Daily Energy Expenditure (TDEE) is estimated from Basal Metabolic Rate
(BMR) multiplied by an activity factor. BMR can be estimated with the
Mifflin-St Jeor equation. For gradual, sustainable fat loss, a deficit of about
15-25% below TDEE is commonly used; for lean muscle gain, a modest surplus of
about 5-15% above TDEE paired with resistance training and adequate protein.

Very aggressive deficits can reduce energy, impair performance, and are harder
to sustain. Individual needs vary; consult a qualified professional for medical
conditions.
