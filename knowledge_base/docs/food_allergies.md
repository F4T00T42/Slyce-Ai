---
title: Food Allergies and Common Allergens
source: https://www.fda.gov / https://www.foodallergy.org
---
A food allergy is an immune response triggered by a specific food protein, which
can range from mild (itching, hives) to severe and life-threatening
(anaphylaxis). It differs from food intolerance, which is typically digestive
and not immune-mediated.

A large share of food-allergic reactions are caused by a small set of foods
often called the major allergens: milk, eggs, fish, shellfish (crustacean),
tree nuts, peanuts, wheat, soybeans, and sesame. Mustard is also a recognized
allergen in some regions.

People with diagnosed allergies should avoid the allergen entirely and read
ingredient labels carefully, since allergens can appear in unexpected dishes.
Cross-contact during preparation is a real risk, so those with severe allergies
should confirm preparation practices with the restaurant. This information is
educational and not a substitute for medical advice.
